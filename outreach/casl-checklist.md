# CASL pre-send checklist

Operational checklist for a cold SMS to a Canadian business number that was posted in public. It covers the "conspicuous publication" path to implied consent. It is not legal advice. If you plan to text at volume, have a Canadian communications lawyer review the process.

CASL (S.C. 2010, c. 23) applies because a text to a phone is a commercial electronic message: an electronic address includes a telephone account (s. 1), and section 6 prohibits a commercial message without consent plus the required content. The standing test for this kind of implied consent is section 10(9)(b). Identification and the unsubscribe mechanism are sections 6 and 11, and the short-message allowance for a link to your contact details is in the CRTC Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations (SOR/2012-36, ss. 2–3).

Implied consent here has no fixed expiry date. It lasts only while the listing still meets the test. It is not the same rule as an existing business relationship (generally two years) or an inquiry (six months).

Copy does not create consent. The templates in [sms-templates.md](sms-templates.md) are usable only after every hard stop below is clear.

## 1. The lead qualifies

All of these are true, or you do not send.

- [ ] The phone number is on a page the person published, or caused to be published. Their ad, their own profile, or their own link page.
- [ ] You can open that page and see the number. A private message, a number from a client, or a number reposted on a review forum does not qualify. An age interstitial is fine. A login wall is not.
- [ ] You are texting that published number, not a different one you found later.
- [ ] The page has no refusal of unsolicited commercial messages. Hard stop examples: "no soliciting," "no promoters," "no service providers," "no web designers," "do not contact me with offers."
- [ ] The page does not lock the number to clients. Treat these as a skip unless a lawyer has told you otherwise: "clients only," "booking inquiries only," "serious inquiries only," "do not text unless you are ready to book."
- [ ] The message is about their practice. A private site, screening, tours, or the domain they own. An unrelated offer fails the relevance test in s. 10(9)(b).
- [ ] They are an adult operating their own practice. If you cannot tell the listing is theirs, or you cannot tell they are 18 or older, skip it.
- [ ] They are not already on your suppression list.
- [ ] You picked one template, not several.

"Business number" here means the phone number they publish for the practice. It does not mean a CRA business number.

A board outage, a ban, or a wish to leave classified ads is not something you need them to have written. Relevance comes from the fact that they publicly advertise a practice that depends on clients being able to find them. The listing still has to be theirs, current, and free of a refusal.

## 2. Log the proof before you send

A URL on its own is weak, because the page can change after you text. Capture the page on the day you send.

Store one record per number:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Checked at | Date, time, and timezone |
| URL | The exact listing you reviewed |
| Screenshot | Full screen, including the number and the text around it, so the absence of a refusal is visible. Do not crop that part out. |
| Number | Exactly as published, plus the E.164 version you dialled |
| Disclaimer check | "None found" only after you read the whole listing, or quote the line that made you skip it |
| Relevance | One sentence on why a website for this practice relates to the business they are advertising |
| Template | 1, 2, 3, 4, follow-up 1, or follow-up 2 |
| Sent at | Date, time, and timezone. Blank if you skipped. |
| Reply | What they said, if anything |
| Opt-out | Date you suppressed the number, and what you did |

Keep the file for at least three years after the last message to that person. Keep it longer if a complaint is open. Re-open the live URL on the morning of every follow-up and save a new screenshot. If the ad is gone, or a refusal line has appeared, do not send.

Suggested file name: `YYYY-MM-DD-city-firstname.png`, stored next to the row, not in a camera roll you will lose.

## 3. The message itself

- [ ] Sender name and business name match the contact page.
- [ ] The link in the text opens that page with no login, and a Canadian mailing address (street or PO box) is visible there.
- [ ] The text includes `Reply STOP to opt out.`
- [ ] The filled message is 320 characters or fewer and uses plain GSM characters. No emoji, no smart quotes.
- [ ] You are sending from a number you control, at a humane hour in their timezone (late morning or early afternoon). That hour is etiquette, not a CASL clause. It keeps a sales text off a line that may be in an appointment.
- [ ] You recounted the filled message, including the real URL.

Section 11 gives you 10 business days as the outside limit to honour an unsubscribe. Do not plan around that limit. Suppress the number before the next send window, and in any case the same day.

## 4. Opt-outs

Treat all of these as a stop: STOP, stop, unsubscribe, remove me, do not text, leave me alone, and a clear "no thanks."

1. Add the number to the suppression list before you do anything else.
2. Cancel any unsent follow-up, including the day-7 note.
3. Do not ask why, and do not send a new pitch.
4. Do not text them from a different number. The opt-out belongs to you and the business, not to one handset.
5. Leave the record in the log.

A confirmation text is legally allowed only if it has no commercial content. On a booking line, silence plus suppression is the better default. If you ever confirm, the whole message is that they will not hear from you again. No link and no offer.

If they reply with a question, answer that question and drop the rest of the sequence. A request for the preview is express interest. A refusal is the end.

## 5. After the first text

- [ ] Day 3: follow-up 1, only if there is no reply, no opt-out, and the listing still qualifies.
- [ ] Day 7: follow-up 2, under the same conditions, then stop.
- [ ] No third follow-up, and no fresh sequence from another number.

Maximum administrative penalties under CASL are $1,000,000 per violation for an individual and $10,000,000 for a business. The useful habit is smaller than that sentence: if the listing makes you argue with yourself, skip it.

## What this checklist does not do

- It does not replace express consent. A reply that asks you to send the work is the clean way to continue.
- It does not let you email a different address, or text a different number, on the back of one published phone number.
- It does not cover purchased lists. Check the live page yourself.
- It is not a review of the Criminal Code rules around advertising sexual services. These templates pitch web design. Keep them on that subject.
